# Part 1 — Group Buy

## 1.1 Headline and explanation

> ### Group Buy
> Join other CaratForUs customers to unlock a lower price on the same piece.
> As more pieces are ordered, the price drops for everyone — including
> everyone who already ordered.

Restates the locked settlement rule (final unlocked tier applies to every
participant, difference refunded). No new right — it is the customer-facing
statement of one you already granted.

Worth keeping rather than trimming: without it, the rational move is to wait,
and waiting is what stops a campaign reaching its next tier.

## 1.2 Tier and progress

> Progress only ever moves forward. If someone later cancels, the price you

Restates locked monotonic progress. No new right.

## 1.3 Payment type selection

Restates locked display rules: Card price leads, one active price at a time,

The 48+48 window, the 96-hour set-aside, immediate progress counting, the

The marked sentence commits us to **sending** the instructions. The locked

established — but it is an operational obligation stated to the customer and

"We'll set your order aside" is my wording for what is internally an inactive
status. A customer reading "deactivated" for a slow bank transfer would
reasonably think they had lost the order, which the policy explicitly does
not intend.

# Part 2 — Warranty

## 2.1 Page intro

> ### Warranty claim
> If something has gone wrong with a CaratForUs piece, tell us what happened
> and we'll review it. Start by finding your order below.

No promise beyond reviewing a submitted claim, which is what the workflow
does.

## 2.2 What the 1-year limited warranty covers

> **What's covered**
> For one year from delivery, we cover manufacturing defects — problems with
> how your piece was made.
>
> ⚠️ **NEW OBLIGATION** — That includes things like a stone coming loose from
> a setting that wasn't damaged, a clasp or finding that fails in normal wear,
> or a soldered joint separating.

The one-year term and "manufacturing defects" are locked. The **examples are
mine** — the policy defines the category without enumerating instances, and a
named example reads to a customer as a guaranteed inclusion.

They are the three most common genuine manufacturing failures in fine
jewellery and I believe they are safe, but they are an interpretation of your
policy rather than a quotation of it. Approve, edit the list, or cut it and
let the category stand alone.

## 2.3 What it is not

> **What isn't covered**
> - Normal wear — surface scratches, dulled polish, thinning over years of
>   daily wear
> - Damage from an impact, accident, or loss
> - Work done by another jeweller after delivery
> - Resizing, alteration or repair not carried out or authorised by us
> - Loss or theft

Each exclusion is locked. The parenthetical examples of normal wear are
illustrative of a locked category and narrow what we exclude rather than
widening it, so I have not marked them — say if you would rather they go.

Nothing here promises a repair, a quote, or a turnaround. The paid-repair-quote
sentence you struck is gone and not replaced.

## 2.4 Do not ship until authorised

> **Please don't send your item yet.**
> Wait until we've reviewed your claim and sent you shipping instructions.
>
> ⚠️ **NEW OBLIGATION** — Items sent before we authorise an inspection can't
> be tracked against your claim and may be returned to you.

The instruction not to ship before authorisation is locked (criterion 156 —
the confirmation must carry no shipping instruction, and a render test asserts
this notice appears in **both** the signed-in and signed-out states so the
sign-in gate cannot suppress it).

The marked sentence states what happens to an unsolicited package. That is an
operational position I invented: it discourages early shipping honestly
rather than leaving a customer to guess, but "may be returned to you" commits
us to a handling behaviour. Strike it if you would rather say nothing, though
a customer who ships early and hears nothing is a worse outcome.

## 2.5 Customer-paid tracked and insured inbound shipping

> **If we authorise an inspection**
> You arrange and pay for shipping to us, and it must be tracked and insured
> for the full value of the piece. We'll send exact instructions with your
> authorisation.
>
> ⚠️ **NEW OBLIGATION (a compression, not an addition)** — Until your package
> reaches us, it's covered by your carrier and insurance rather than by us.

Customer-paid, tracked, appropriately insured inbound shipping after
authorisation is locked.

The marked sentence states risk-of-loss plainly and **omits the policy's
"subject to applicable law" qualifier**, which belongs on the linked policy
page. I flagged the same compression in the RMA acknowledgment. It only ever
operates in the customer's favour, so the plain wording is the honest one for
a page a customer skims — but removing a legal hedge is your call and
arguably your counsel's, so it is marked rather than assumed.

## 2.8 Confirmation

> **Claim received — reference {claimReference}**
> ⚠️ **NEW OBLIGATION** — We've got it. We'll review what you've sent and
> email you at {purchaserEmail}.
>
> Please don't send your item yet — we'll tell you if and when an inspection
> is needed, and how to ship it.

No turnaround time, per your instruction.

The marked sentence commits us to emailing an outcome. The workflow does email
claim status, so this is close to established, but it is an operational
obligation stated to the customer. The second paragraph restates §2.4 and is
locked.
